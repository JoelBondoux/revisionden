# Content accuracy

Version: 1.0
Effective date: {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}

## In plain English

- Every question in RevisionDen is checked before you see it, and is linked to official material
  from the exam board or the government.
- Each question goes through several separate checks, including a check by a person.
- If you think a question is wrong, tap **Report a problem**. The question is hidden from
  everyone straight away until it's checked.
- If a question really was wrong, it's fixed and your scores are worked out again.
- No checking can promise zero mistakes, but this process is built to catch them.
- If the summary and the full text seem to differ, the full text is the one that counts.

## Full text

### 1. Our standard

1.1 {{OPERATOR_NAME}} aims for no mistakes in RevisionDen's educational content: questions,
answers, explanations, diagrams, flashcards and mark schemes. No process can guarantee that, so
ours is designed so that an error has to pass several independent checks before a student sees
it, and any error that gets through is removed and traced quickly.

### 2. Sources and citations

2.1 Every item carries at least one citation, to an official source wherever one exists: exam
board specifications, mark schemes, examiner reports and sample assessments; then government and
regulator documents (Department for Education subject content, Ofqual); then other authoritative
sources, which need human approval. Commercial revision sites, forums and AI output are never
used as sources.

2.2 Citations include a short quote from the source and where it appears, and are checked
automatically against the official document. They are kept with the content and are not shown in
the app; an audit report can be produced for any item.

2.3 Exam boards' own documents always take priority. RevisionDen is not affiliated with or endorsed
by any exam board.

### 3. Checks before release

3.1 Every item passes these checks before it can be shown to students:

1. it is written only from the cited source excerpts;
2. every quote is found in the source at the stated place;
3. a second AI model from a different provider answers it without seeing our answer, and must
   agree (for extended answers, it marks example answers instead);
4. a separate review looks for errors, ambiguity, a second correct option, or content outside the
   specification;
5. exact checks: every maths answer is checked by our maths engine, and units, chemical equations
   and equation-sheet facts are checked by code where they apply;
6. automatic checks of the content format and links;
7. human approval, or, for large batches, a human check of a random sample of at least 10% (and
   at least 30 items) with zero errors. Extended-answer items always need individual approval.

3.2 Only items that pass every check are shown to students. Draft items are never shown outside
development.

3.3 Diagrams are drawn by code from checked data. We do not use AI-generated images for teaching.

### 4. Reporting a mistake

4.1 Students and parents can tap **Report a problem** on any question and choose a reason: wrong
answer, wrong explanation, unclear, typo, not on my course, or other. No free text is collected,
so a report cannot contain personal details. The report records the question, its version, the
reason, whether a student or parent sent it, and the family.

4.2 The question is hidden from every student at once.

4.3 A reviewer then checks it. If there is no error, the question comes back. If there is an
error, it stays hidden until a corrected version is released, students' scores and mastery for
that question are worked out again, and the related questions (from the same batch, prompt or
source) are checked again.

4.4 Reviewers are recorded by role only (for example subject specialist), never by name.
