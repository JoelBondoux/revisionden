## In plain English

This notice is for parents and carers. There is a shorter version written for students in the
[privacy notice for young people](privacy-for-young-people.md). If this summary and the full text
ever seem to say different things, the full text is the one that counts.

- **Who we are.** RevisionDen is run by {{OPERATOR_NAME}}. You can contact us at
  {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.
- **What we keep about you.** Only the email address you sign in with (with a Google or
  Microsoft account, whichever this app offers), and the records the sign-in needs to work. We do not keep your name or photo.
  If a second parent joins your family, we keep their sign-in email in the same way.
- **Two parents.** A family can have two parents with equal control. A family activity log shows
  what each changed, as "Parent 1" or "Parent 2", never by name.
- **What we keep about your child.** A nickname, an avatar, their school year, their subjects and
  exam boards, and their revision records: which questions they answered, whether they were right,

  real name, date of birth, address, phone number, email, school name, location or photos.
- **Why.** To run the revision tutor you asked for: pick the right questions, mark answers, plan
  revision and keep your child motivated. Nothing else. No adverts, no selling data, no tracking.
- **AI marking.** Some written answers are sent to an AI service to help mark them. We remove
  anything that could identify your child first, and we only use paid plans whose terms say
  the content is not used to train the AI. Some of these companies are in the United States (see the
  [list of services we use](processors.md)).

- **How long.** Most data stays until you delete it. You can delete your child's data at any
  time. Accounts nobody uses for 12 months are deleted automatically. See
  [how long we keep data](data-retention.md).
- **Your rights.** You can ask to see, correct, download or delete your family's data, and you
  can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). Your child has these rights too.

## Full text

### 1. About this notice

1.1 This privacy notice explains how {{OPERATOR_NAME}} ("we", "us") collects and uses personal
data when you and your children use RevisionDen (the "app"), a revision tutor for students
preparing for GCSE and A-Level exams in the UK.

1.2 It is written for parents and carers who hold parental responsibility for a student using the
app. A version written for students aged 13 to 18 is at
[privacy notice for young people](privacy-for-young-people.md). Both describe the same processing.

1.3 We have written this notice to meet our duties to be transparent under the UK General Data
Protection Regulation ("UK GDPR") and the Data Protection Act 2018, and to follow the Information
Commissioner's Office (ICO) Age Appropriate Design Code (the "Children's Code"). Where the plain
English summary above and this full text differ, this full text applies.

1.4 This notice is a draft. It has not yet been reviewed by a qualified solicitor and must be
reviewed before the app is offered to anyone outside the operator's own family.

### 2. Who is responsible for your data

2.1 {{OPERATOR_NAME}}, a sole trader, is the controller of the personal data described in this
notice.

2.2 Contact: {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}. Please use this for any question about
privacy or to use any of your rights.

2.3 We have not appointed a data protection officer. We do not believe we are required to; you
can still contact us about anything in this notice at the details above.

### 3. Who uses the app

3.1 **Parents and carers.** One parent or carer creates a family account by signing in with a
Google or Microsoft account (whichever of the two this app offers). The parent adds each child, chooses their subjects and links their
devices.

3.2 **Students.** Students do not have their own outside account, email address or password.
A parent creates a profile for each student with a nickname, an avatar and a school year, and
links the student's device with a one-time QR code. The student then unlocks the app on that
device with a passkey (the device's own fingerprint, face or PIN check). The app is designed for
students in Years 9 to 13 (usually aged 13 to 18).

3.3 The app is not designed for children under 13 and we do not knowingly provide it to them.

### 4. The personal data we collect

4.1 **About the parent or carer**

- the email address of the Google or Microsoft account used to sign in, and whether that provider
  says it is verified;
- the identifier the sign-in provider gives your account (see 4.5);
- your sign-in sessions (a random token and an expiry time). We switch off the sign-in library's
  recording of IP address and browser details, so sessions do not store them;
- the versions of the terms of use and this notice that you have confirmed, and when;
- when the family account was last used by you or your children (so that accounts nobody uses
  for 12 months can be deleted);
- feedback or bug reports you choose to send us (see section 9);
- reports of a mistake in a question (only the question, its version and a reason picked from a
  list; no free text);
- if you have been given a reviewer role for checking questions, that role;
- if your family has a second parent: the same records about them (their own sign-in email,
  provider identifier, sessions and policy confirmations), and whether each of you is "Parent 1"
  or "Parent 2";
- the family activity log: which changes were made (for example a child added, a reward given or
  the data downloaded), by "Parent 1" or "Parent 2", and when. It never holds a parent's name or
  email. Entries about a child are deleted with the child, and entries are kept for 12 months;
- an invite code for a second parent, stored only as a scrambled (hashed) value, valid for 7 days
  and usable once;
- if one parent removes the other, a note that the removed parent was removed and when, kept only
  until they have seen it in the app.

4.1a **Two parents.** Either parent can invite a second parent, remove the other parent (who then
loses access at once and sees a notice the next time they sign in), and download or delete the
family's data. Deleting the whole family account needs both parents to confirm. Each parent is
told in the app, and by push notification if they turned it on, about important changes the other
parent makes: children added or removed, rewards given, limits changed, data downloaded and
requests to delete the family account. These notifications say "Parent 1" or "Parent 2", never a
name.

We do not store your name or profile picture. The app asks the sign-in provider for a minimum
profile and blanks the name and picture before anything is saved.

4.2 **About each student**

- **Profile:** nickname (2 to 16 letters, numbers or underscores, which must not be a real name),
  avatar (saved as a short code describing the drawing), and school year;
- **Courses:** subjects, exam boards, tiers and specification versions;
- **Devices:** for each linked device, the public part of its passkey and technical details the
  passkey needs (an identifier, a counter and how the device connects), when it was linked and
  last used; one-time link codes and sessions are stored only as scrambled (hashed) values;
- **Learning records:** each answer's question, whether it was right, the score, how long it
  took, the mode (learn or test), the XP earned and when; topic mastery scores; starting test
  records; daily XP; streaks, skip days and gift days; rewards earned; flashcards and their review
  schedule, including the text of any flashcards the student writes themselves (refused if it
  holds an email address, a UK mobile number or a nickname from your family); study plans;
  exam, mock and assessment dates and results that are entered;
- **Reminders:** if the student turns reminders on, the web push address and keys that the
  student's browser gives us, the reminder settings the parent chooses, and a log of reminders
  sent (the kind, the date and our own wording);
- **AI usage:** for each AI call made for the student, the job, the AI provider and model, the
  number of tokens and the cost. Never the question, the answer or the AI's reply;
- feedback the student chooses to send us, and reports of a mistake in a question.

4.3 **What we do not collect.** We do not collect or store a student's real name, date of birth,
home address, phone number, email address, photographs, location, contacts or school name. The
database is checked by an automatic test that fails if a column that could hold these is added.
We do not store the text of students' answers to questions; an answer is used to mark the
question and then only the result is kept.

4.4 **Data used for a moment but not stored.** To mark some written answers, the text of the
answer is sent to an AI provider (section 7). To check worked maths, the lines of working are
sent to our maths engine. Neither is stored in our database. Your and your child's internet
address (IP address) is seen by our hosting provider and used for a moment to limit the number of
requests (to protect the app from abuse); we do not store it.

4.5 **Sign-in tokens.** When you sign in, Google or Microsoft give the app some tokens: keys to
your account with them and a signed message (the "ID token") that can contain the name and
picture on that account. We use them only for that moment to learn your email address and account
identifier, and we do not keep any of them: they are blanked before the sign-in record is saved.
The account identifier is all the app needs to recognise you next time, and we never use your
Google or Microsoft account for anything else.

4.6 **Features not available yet.** Some features described in our plans are not yet built,
including reading photos of homework or school reports, marking long answers and essays, and
friends. Where this notice describes them, it says so. We will update this notice, and tell you
in the app, before any of them starts collecting data.

5.3 **Consent.** We do not rely on consent as our main lawful basis, so the rules in the UK GDPR
on a child's consent to online services do not decide whether a student may use the app. Where
the law requires consent for storing or reading information on a device, the only storage we use
is strictly necessary (see [cookies and storage](cookies-and-storage.md)), which does not need
consent.

5.4 **Children and parental responsibility.** The app is set up and controlled by a parent or
carer with parental responsibility, who agrees to our terms of use. Students are aged 13 to 18.
We treat a student as able to understand and use their own data protection rights unless it is
clear they cannot, and we explain the app to them in the
[privacy notice for young people](privacy-for-young-people.md). Because students are children, we
have weighed our legitimate interests against their interests, rights and freedoms with their
best interests as a primary consideration, following the Children's Code. A written record of that
balancing test and a data protection impact assessment are still to be completed before the app
is offered beyond the operator's family (see [the Children's Code page](childrens-code.md)).

5.5 **Special category data.** We do not ask for or intend to process special category data
(for example health, religion or ethnicity). Please do not include it in feedback or in the text
of flashcards.

5.6 **Automated decisions.** The app adapts automatically to how the student is doing: it picks
the next questions, sets a feedback level and shows predicted progress. AI is used to suggest
marks for some written answers. None of these decisions has a legal effect or a similarly
significant effect on the student; AI marks are always labelled as a guide and are not exam
grades. See [AI and marking](ai-and-marking.md).

### 6. Who we share data with

6.1 We use a small number of service providers ("processors") who handle data for us under our
instructions: hosting and storage, the database, the maths engine, AI marking, sign-in and push
reminders. The full list, what each receives and where, is in
[services we use](processors.md).

6.2 Within a family, the parent can see their children's profiles, courses, devices, AI usage and
feedback, and (once the parent dashboard is built) their progress.
Students are told that their parent can see their activity.

6.3 We do not share data with schools, exam boards, advertisers or data brokers. We may disclose
data if the law requires it, for example to comply with a court order.

### 7. AI marking and what the AI provider receives

7.1 Short written answers are marked first by fixed rules. Only if the rules cannot decide, the
question, the topic, the student's answer and the relevant official mark points are sent to an AI
model (currently Anthropic's Claude) through Cloudflare's AI Gateway.

7.2 Before any request is sent, our server checks the exact text that would be sent and refuses
it if it contains the student's nickname, their internal identifiers, their avatar code, their
school year written as "Year" and a number, an email address or a UK mobile phone number. The
student is asked to leave out personal details. The AI Gateway is set not to keep the content of
requests or replies in its logs.

7.3 We use paid API services only, because the providers' published terms for paid API use say
that customer content is not used to train their models by default. AI providers may keep request content for a limited period
under their own terms (for example to detect abuse); see [services we use](processors.md).

7.4 Even with these checks, an answer a student types is still personal data, because it was
written by them. Please remind your child not to write their name or other personal details in
answers.

### 8. Where data is stored and international transfers

8.1 Our database is hosted by Neon in its London region. Our app and server run on Cloudflare's
global network, which handles each request at a data centre close to the user; this can be outside
the UK.

8.2 Some processors are based in, or process data in, the United States. In particular, the AI
model provider (Anthropic) may process the question and answer text in the United States, and
Cloudflare, Neon, Google and Microsoft are US-based companies. We keep stored data (the database
and uploads) in the UK or EU. The text sent for AI marking, which contains nothing that identifies
the student, may be processed in the United States; we have decided to accept this and to rely on
a transfer safeguard for it (see 8.3).

8.3 Where personal data is transferred outside the UK, the UK GDPR requires a safeguard, such as
UK adequacy regulations (including the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework for
companies certified under it), the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum
to the EU standard contractual clauses. We are confirming which safeguard applies to each
provider and will list it in [services we use](processors.md). Until that is done, the app must
not be offered beyond the operator's family.

### 9. Feedback and bug reports

9.1 Parents and students can send us feedback (a bug, an idea, something confusing or other) with
optional text of up to 500 characters. Before it is saved, the text is checked and refused if it
contains an email address, a UK mobile phone number or a nickname from your family.

9.2 You can choose to include device details: the app version, the screen you were on, the
browser family, the screen size and whether you were online. For students this starts switched
off; for parents it starts switched on and can be turned off. We never collect your IP address,
location or contacts with feedback.

9.3 Parents can see their family's feedback and its status. Feedback is never sent automatically
to any other service. If we copy a report into our own issue tracker, we remove anything that
could identify your family. Feedback is deleted 12 months after we close it.

### 10. How long we keep data

10.1 We keep data only as long as we need it. Most data is kept while the family account is used
and deleted when you delete it. If nobody in the family uses the account for 12 months, it is
deleted automatically with everything in it; if you sign in after 11 months or more without use,
the app warns you. The full table is in [how long we keep data](data-retention.md).

### 11. Security

11.1 We use encrypted connections (HTTPS) everywhere. Our hosting and database providers encrypt
stored data. There are no passwords: parents sign in with a Google or Microsoft account (whichever this app offers) and students use
passkeys. Link codes and student sessions are stored only as hashes. Each family can only see its
own data, and our tests check this.

### 12. Your rights

12.1 Under the UK GDPR you have the right to:

- be informed about how we use your data (this notice);
- access the data we hold about you and receive a copy;
- have inaccurate data corrected;
- have data erased;
- restrict how we use data;
- data portability (receive data you gave us in a common machine-readable format);
- object to processing based on legitimate interests, including our use of a child's data;
- not be subject to decisions based solely on automated processing that have legal or similarly
  significant effects (we do not make such decisions).

12.2 **Students' rights.** These rights belong to the student for their own data. A parent may
use them on a child's behalf. Where a student is mature enough to understand their rights, we will
usually respond to the student directly and consider their wishes, and we will not let a parent
use the rights against the student's best interests.

12.3 **How to use your rights.** In the app, a parent can change or delete a student's profile and
unlink devices at any time. Deleting a student removes their profile and all their learning
records. For anything else, contact us at {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}. We will reply within one month, which
the law lets us extend in some cases; we will tell you if we do. There is normally no charge.

12.4 In the parent area, "Download your family's data" gives you a file (in the common JSON
format) with everything we hold about you and your children. It leaves out only security codes
such as scrambled session codes and device keys. In the parent home, "Delete the family account"
deletes the whole family, every child's records and both parents' sign-ins; when there are two
parents, the other parent must confirm it first.
